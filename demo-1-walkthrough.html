02

Policy briefing for young leaders

Compare functions, not labels

Ask who classifies risk, what evidence is required, who can inspect it, and what happens after failure.

Procurement is the common lever

Contracts can require audit access, change notices, data provenance, testing evidence, and safe exit rights.

Remedies remain the hardest gap

A governance checklist is not equivalent to a person’s enforceable right to challenge an outcome.

03

Realistic trilateral alignment

  1. 1
    Common public-sector accountability record

    Share minimum fields for purpose, decision role, affected population, data provenance, evaluation, human authority, incidents, and appeal routes.

  2. 2
    Model procurement clauses

    Align audit cooperation, model-change notices, performance reporting, incident notification, portability, and termination rights.

  3. 3
    Evaluation and incident exchange

    Pilot common test scenarios and incident taxonomies without pooling sensitive operational or personal data.

04

Implementation memo

  1. 30
    Days 0–30 · Fix the comparison frame

    Appoint a lead in each country, agree on the accountability-record fields, and choose one public-sector use case per jurisdiction.

  2. 90
    Days 31–90 · Draft and test controls

    Prepare model contract clauses and test whether audit access, change notices, human review, and appeal routes can be recorded consistently.

  3. 180
    Days 91–180 · Run the pilot

    Conduct a joint tabletop exercise, compare evaluation and incident results, revise the record, and publish lessons.
